Know Your Rights

Tap a heading to read. This is a simple summary. Details can differ by state and situation.

Fundamental rights
  • Equality before the law and no discrimination (Articles 14 to 18).
  • Freedom of speech, movement and work, and protection of life and personal liberty (Articles 19 to 22).
  • No forced labour or child labour in dangerous work (Articles 23 and 24).
  • Freedom of religion, and cultural and educational rights (Articles 25 to 30).
  • The right to go to the High Court or Supreme Court if your rights are violated (Article 32).
Consumer rights
  • Safe goods and services, and honest information about them.
  • The right to choose, to be heard, and to seek repair, replacement, refund or compensation.
  • You can complain to the Consumer Commission, often without a lawyer.
Women's rights
  • Protection from domestic violence and dowry harassment.
  • A safe workplace, free from sexual harassment, with a complaints committee.
  • Equal pay for equal work, and maternity benefits for working women.
  • You can file a Zero FIR at any police station.
Children's rights
  • Free and compulsory education from age 6 to 14.
  • Protection from abuse and exploitation. Help is available on CHILDLINE 1098.
  • Protection from child labour and unsafe work.
Employee and labour rights
  • Wages on time, and not less than the minimum wage.
  • Written terms of employment, and a safe workplace.
  • Provident fund and insurance benefits where they apply.
Online safety rights
  • You can report online fraud, threats, stalking and fake accounts. Call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in.
  • You can ask for private or intimate images to be taken down.
  • Never share your OTP, PIN or password with anyone.

Mediation

What it is: Mediation is a conversation where a neutral person helps two sides reach an agreement they both accept. The mediator does not decide who is right.

When it suits: Money disputes, rent and deposit problems, neighbour or family disagreements, and consumer or workplace conflicts, where both sides are willing to talk. It is usually faster and cheaper than court. Free mediation is available through Legal Services Authorities and Lok Adalats.

Not suitable for: threats, abuse, domestic violence, stalking, sexual offences or any serious crime. If you are unsafe, call 112 and seek help instead. Never agree to meet someone who is threatening you.
